\textbf{Question:} \\

 A square matrix $\vec{B}$ is skew-symmetric if 
\begin{multicols}{4}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*)]
    \item  $\vec{B}^\top = -\vec{B}$ 
    \item  $\vec{B}^\top = \vec{B}$ 
    \item  $\vec{B}^{-1} = \vec{B}$ 
    \item  $\vec{B}^{-1} = \vec{B}^\top$ 
\end{enumerate}
\end{multicols}

\textbf{Solution:}\\

 $\vec{B}$ is  skew-symmetric, if and only if 
 \begin{align}
     \vec{B}^\top = -\vec{B}
 \end{align}

Example: 
\begin{align}
\vec{B} &= \myvec{0 & 2 & -1 \\ -2 & 0 & 3 \\ 1 & -3 & 0} \quad \text{is a skew symmetric matrix}\\
&\implies \vec{B}^\top = \myvec{0 & -2 & 1 \\ 2 & 0 & -3 \\ -1 & 3 & 0} = -\vec{B}
\end{align}
